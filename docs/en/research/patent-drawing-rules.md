# Patent drawing rules: DPMA, EPO, PCT/WIPO, USPTO

Research status: 2026-11-20. Primary sources only. Where a value could not be confirmed in an official text it is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Rule IDs (for example `EP-07`) are referenced by the preflight ideas in section 5.

Abbreviations: M = mandatory ("shall/must"), R = recommended ("should/preferably"), Auto = can be checked automatically (yes/partly/no).

> Key corrections to common assumptions
> - **Rule 46 EPC no longer exists.** It was deleted with effect from 1 Feb 2023 (CA/D 10/22, OJ EPO 2022, A101). Drawing form is now set by the **President's decision under Rule 49(2) EPC**. Since 1 Oct 2025 that decision is **OJ EPO 2025, A49**.
> - **Since 1 Oct 2025 the EPO accepts colour and greyscale drawings, but only when they are filed electronically** (OJ EPO 2025, A49 Art. 1(2)(a); A57). PCT applications remain black-and-white.
> - **PCT Rule 11.13(a) is unchanged in the Regulations in force from 1 Jan 2026** ("without colorings"). In 2026 WIPO only *proposed* publishing additional colour PDFs (C. PCT 1699).
> - **USPTO drawings must not contain centre lines or projection lines** (37 CFR 1.84(h)). EPO and PCT guidance explicitly lists dot-dash lines for axes.
> - **Every office uses the same drawing-sheet margins** on A4 (top 2.5, left 2.5, right 1.5, bottom 1.0 cm; usable area 26.2 × 17 cm). Only the margins for text pages differ.
> - **DPMA:** For electronic filing, PDF drawings must be black and white, uncompressed, and use only the fonts Times, Helvetica, Courier and Symbol. The DPMA information sheet also says photographs are *not* drawings within the meaning of § 12 PatV.

## 2. Harmonised "safe common profile" (strictest common denominator)

This profile is accepted by DPMA, EPO, PCT and USPTO for utility and patent filings.

| Parameter | Safe value | Driven by |
|---|---|---|
| Page | A4 portrait, 210 × 297 mm. Rotate figures, not pages. | PCT-01, US-12 (portrait display) |
| Margins (min) | top 25, left 25, right 15, bottom 10 mm | all (DE-02, EP-03, PCT-02, US-07) |
| Usable area | ≤ 170 × 262 mm. **No border or frame.** | all |
| Margin content | Empty. Optional: applicant file reference top-left within 15 mm of the top edge (PCT). | PCT-02 |
| Line colour | Pure black (#000000) on white. No grey, no colour, no anti-aliased grey edges in raster export. | DE-05, PCT-05, US-02 |
| Fills | No solid black areas (except bar graphs). No grey fills. Shading only as thin spaced lines. | US-15, DE-04 |

**Per-office deviations (where the profile can be relaxed):**
- **EPO:** colour and greyscale allowed for electronic filings from 1 Oct 2025 (EP-04/05). The label prefix "FIG" without a period is also fine. Dot-dash axes and detail circles are allowed.
- **PCT:** sheet number may also sit bottom centre. Dot-dash lines for axes and symmetry are explicitly listed (PCT-14). Colour is converted to B/W.
- **USPTO:** Letter paper allowed, but the sight is then 176 × 244 mm (smaller height). Scan cross-hairs recommended. Optional identification header in the top margin. Colour by petition (utility) or freely (design). Exploded views must use brackets (US-09).
- **DPMA:** alternative abstract-figure area 81 × 94 mm or 174 × 45 mm (DE-03). Prior-art figures labelled "Stand der Technik". Restricted PDF fonts. Sheet-number format not prescribed.

## 3. What patent drawings look like (conventions)

These conventions come from the cited rules (PCT Guide 5.144–5.158, 37 CFR 1.84, EPO GL A-IX) plus common practice. Points without a rule are marked as practice.

- **Line styles.** Visible edges use continuous thick lines. Reference lines, hatching and adjacent parts use thin continuous lines. Hidden edges use thin short dashes. Axes, symmetry planes and extreme positions of moving parts use thin dot-dash lines. Break lines are thin and freehand (Guide 5.144). The USPTO forbids centre lines and projection lines (1.84(h)) and reserves broken lines for alternate positions (1.84(h)(4)). Design patents use broken lines for environment only (1.152).
- **Section views.** The section plane is shown as a broken line in the parent view, ends marked with the figure number (often Roman, e.g. "XXII–XXII") and arrows for viewing direction. Cut faces are hatched at 45°, with a different angle for each adjacent part. Large areas are hatched along the edge only. The hatching is interrupted around numerals.
- **Exploded views.** Parts are separated along the assembly axis. The USPTO requires a bracket embracing the separated parts (1.84(h)(1)).
- **Perspective and isometric views** are explicitly allowed (DE-06, EP-19, US-08). Shading uses thin surface lines (parallel lines on cylinders, concentric arcs on spheres) with light from the upper left at 45° (1.84(m)). No solid fills, and stippling only sparsely (practice).
- **Lead lines** are thin, short, and straight or curved. A wavy or kinked form is common practice to separate them from object lines (practice). They start next to the numeral, end on the feature (an optional arrow means a whole assembly), and never cross each other (US). An underlined numeral without a lead line designates the surface it sits on (1.84(q); Guide 5.145).
- **Reference numerals** are placed outside the object where possible, aligned with the view, and spaced from lines. Series by embodiment (115/215) are allowed (EP GL A-IX 7.5.5). Primes (12') are accepted for variants.
- **Arrows:** a free-standing arrow marks a whole assembly or section. An arrow touching a line marks the surface seen along the arrow. Movement arrows show a direction of motion (1.84(r)).
- **FIG labels** are larger than numerals and sit below or beside each view. Partial or alternative views use a letter suffix ("FIG. 3A").
- **Broken views:** long parts are interrupted with freehand or zigzag break lines (Guide 5.144). Very long views may be split and stacked on one sheet with a clear relationship (1.84(h)(2)(ii); EP GL A-IX 5.3).
- **Detail (enlarged) views:** the area is circled with a thin or dot-dash circle in the parent figure and drawn as a separately numbered figure (EP GL A-IX 7.6; 1.84(h)(2)).
- **Flowcharts:** boxes and decision diamonds carry short step texts. A short sentence per step is tolerated at the EPO (GL A-IX 8). Steps are numbered like reference signs, e.g. S100, S102, … or 100, 102, … The step numbering is practice, not a rule. Start and end are terminators. Arrows show flow direction.
- **Block diagrams:** labelled rectangles for conventional components ("labeled rectangular box", 1.83(a)), joined by lines or arrows. Each block also carries a reference numeral.
- **Graphs and waveforms:** axes labelled briefly, word reading left-to-right except axis labels (1.84(i)). A waveform group forms one figure with a common time axis and per-trace letters (1.84(d)). Bars may be solid black (1.84(m)).
- **Circuit and fluid schematics:** standard symbols (IEC 60617, ISO 1219), explained in the description if not universally recognised (1.84(n); EP GL A-IX 9).

**Licensing implications for an open-source library**
- ISO states that all ISO publications are copyright protected and that copying "in whole or in part" requires written permission (https://www.iso.org/copyright.html). The IEC 60617 database is subscription-only and marked "All rights reserved" (TC 3 page above).
- Safe approach:
  1. Draw every symbol independently from a functional description. Do not trace, scan or extract vector data from standard PDFs or the IEC database.
  2. Reference standard *numbers* and generic names only. Do not copy symbol descriptions, figure captions, tables or IEC/ISO registration IDs in bulk. In the EU, systematic extraction from the IEC database may also touch the sui generis database right (legal assessment **UNVERIFIED**, not legal advice).
  3. Mark the library as "inspired by / compatible with conventions of …", not "conforming to ISO xxxx", unless it has been checked against a licensed copy.
  4. Prefer symbols that are already conventional and simple geometric forms. Patent offices require such symbols to be universally recognised (EP GL A-IX 9; 1.84(n)).
  5. Licence your own drawings under the project licence and keep provenance notes per symbol.
